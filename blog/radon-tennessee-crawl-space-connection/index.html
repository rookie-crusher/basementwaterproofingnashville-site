Radon in Tennessee: why Middle Tennessee tests high, and what sealing a crawl space does about it

Tennessee tests high for radon. The state's own radon programme reports that 16 percent of Tennessee homes surveyed had elevated levels against about 6 percent nationally, and in some counties the share ran between 33 and 75 percent. The driver is geology: uranium-bearing rock decaying underground, with soil gas moving up through fractured limestone into whatever sits above it.

Sealing a crawl space changes the routes that gas uses. It is not a radon system, and this article is careful about that line, because code bodies are not casual about it either. Washington State amended its version of the crawl space provision so that sealing the ground with a Class I vapor retarder also triggers a mandatory radon system. The base IRC R408.3 attaches no such requirement; WAC 51-51-0408 does. We found that by reading the two side by side.

What follows: why the numbers here are what they are, what testing actually involves, where sealing helps and where it does not, and the honest limit of what a waterproofing contractor should claim.

Why is radon high in Tennessee?

Radon is a radioactive gas produced by the decay of uranium in rock and soil. It has no colour, smell or taste, and the EPA sets an action level of 4.0 picocuries per litre, advising you to fix a home at or above that figure and to consider fixing between 2 and 4.

Two things concentrate it here.

The rock

Middle Tennessee sits on fractured, soluble limestone. Karst terrain is full of solution channels and voids, and those act as conduits, so gas produced at depth can travel further and arrive at a foundation rather than dispersing through dense soil. Davidson County is classified by the EPA in Zone 1, its highest predicted radon potential category.

The houses

Crawl space foundations are the regional norm, for reasons covered in house foundation types in Middle Tennessee. A vented crawl space over bare earth gives soil gas a large uncovered surface to enter through, and the stack effect then draws it up into the rooms above along with the moisture. The combination of the geology and that foundation type is why the state figure sits where it does.

One caution about maps. The EPA is explicit that the Map of Radon Zones exists to help organisations target resources and should not be used to decide whether an individual home needs testing. Elevated homes have been found in all three zones. Your neighbour's result does not predict yours either, because the gas follows paths that vary house to house.

How do you test for radon?

Testing is cheap, and it is the only thing that tells you your number.

Short-term and long-term kits

A short-term kit measures over 2 to 90 days. A long-term kit runs beyond 90 days and better reflects a year-round average, because levels swing with season and weather. Radon typically reads higher in winter, when the house is closed and the stack effect is strongest, which is why the state programme recommends testing during consistently cold weather, roughly October to March.

Placement changes the answer

Put the kit in the lowest lived-in level, roughly 3 feet off the floor, away from exterior walls, windows, vents and drafts. Follow closed-house conditions in the instructions. A kit placed in a draft or next to a vent produces a number that means nothing.

The Tennessee Department of Environment and Conservation runs a radon programme and has offered free test kits; check the current TDEC radon page for what is available now. For a real estate transaction or a commercial building, a measurement professional rather than a mail-in kit is the appropriate route, because the protocol and the documentation matter.

Does sealing a crawl space reduce radon?

It reduces some entry pathways. Whether it brings a specific house below 4.0 pCi/L is a question only a test answers, before and after.

Encapsulation covers the exposed earth with a continuous sealed liner, runs it up the stem wall, and seals every penetration: plumbing stacks, duct boots, piers, support posts. Those are the same openings soil gas uses, and the sealing requirements are specific about each one. Closing them is genuine work on the problem.

What it is not is a radon mitigation system. Mitigation, in the form usually specified for a crawl space, is sub-membrane depressurisation: a fan pulling continuously from beneath the liner and discharging above the roofline, so the space under the membrane is held at slightly lower pressure than the house and the gas has somewhere to go that is not your living room. That is a separate design, a separate trade, and it is verified by post-mitigation testing.

Two honest statements about the relationship:

Encapsulation done properly creates the sealed plane that sub-membrane depressurisation needs to work against. On many crawl spaces it is the prerequisite rather than the alternative.

Encapsulation done without testing can also mask a change. Sealing alters pressure relationships in a house, and pressure is what drives soil gas entry. The direction of that change is not something to assume. Test before, test after.

We do crawl space encapsulation, sealing and moisture control across Middle Tennessee. We do not sell radon measurement or mitigation, and we will tell you when what you are describing needs a certified radon professional instead of us. If your crawl space has standing water, bare earth and open penetrations, that is our work, and an inspection will tell you what condition the space is actually in. What your radon number is remains a question for a test.

What about apartments and commercial buildings?

The physics is the same and the engineering is not.

Larger buildings already move air mechanically and set pressure relationships between zones, so a fan added without understanding the existing balance can move gas between units rather than out of the building. Multifamily testing also has to account for units on different levels and above different foundation types.

For an apartment building or a commercial property in Nashville, the sequence is a measurement professional first, a designer who works on buildings of that scale second. Do not scale up a single-family approach. The one piece that carries over unchanged is the sealing of soil gas entry routes in below-grade and crawl space areas, which is ordinary construction work and is where a waterproofing contractor legitimately contributes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a dangerous radon level?

The EPA advises fixing a home at or above 4.0 pCi/L and considering action between 2 and 4. The World Health Organization uses a lower reference of about 2.7 pCi/L. There is no level that is known to be entirely without risk, which is why the guidance is written as an action threshold rather than a safety line.

Is radon worse in a crawl space or a basement?

Both are entry points, and which is worse depends on the house. A vented crawl space over bare earth offers a large uncovered soil surface for gas to enter. A basement has more below-grade wall area and cracks, plus the joint between wall and footing. What decides the indoor number is the pressure relationship between the soil, that space, and the rooms above.

Does a vapor barrier stop radon?

It reduces one pathway rather than stopping the gas. A sealed liner and sealed penetrations cut the routes soil gas uses to enter, but reduction has to be measured rather than assumed, and a liner is not a substitute for a mitigation system where one is needed.

How much does radon testing cost in Tennessee?

A mail-in short-term kit is inexpensive, and TDEC has offered free kits through its radon programme. Professional measurement during a real estate transaction generally runs a couple of hundred dollars in this market and produces documented results in a few days. Check the state programme for current kit availability before buying one.

When should I test for radon?

Ideally during consistently cold weather, roughly October through March, with the house kept under closed conditions. That window produces a result closest to real exposure, because doors and windows are shut and the stack effect is strongest. Testing outside that window is still worthwhile, provided closed-house conditions are followed.

Do I need to test again after crawl space encapsulation?

Yes. Sealing changes both the entry pathways and the pressure relationships in the house, and neither the size nor the direction of the change is safe to assume. A short-term test before the work and another after it is the only way to know what the work did to your number.

Is Davidson County a high radon area?

The EPA places Davidson County in Zone 1, its highest predicted radon potential category. That describes an area, not a house. The EPA states plainly that the zone map should not be used to decide whether an individual home needs testing, because elevated results occur in all three zones.
